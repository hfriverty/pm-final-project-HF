## Read the evidence in your case
- **Where does your case say people stall or leave? Does that match the friction you wrote in Module 2?:** Mara leaves in the middle of the journey, at the two steps where she has to give something: the affordability questions and the income document upload.

About 1,400 people leave at the affordability questions and about 1,300 at the document upload. Together that is more than half of everyone who drops out inside the journey. The document step is the clearest case: a third of people who are asked for a document never get past it.

For Mara, who has two claims and shift income, it gets worse along the way. She is more likely to leave at every step, and only about 31% of people like her end up with an agreement.
- **What got better or worse in the case, and does that support the workaround your persona uses?:** What got better: More people reach an agreement, and fewer call an agent. Both improved steadily across all six months.

What got worse: More plans break, and the jump comes at Month 4 (15.6% to 18.4%), when the 6-month plan became the first option. That is timing-correlated, not proven causal.

The headline numbers hide the problem. Agreement rose by 2.9 points, but plans that actually held rose by only 0.7 points per 100 starters. Most of the gain in agreements is plans that later broke.
- **Which user in the case is carrying the pain, and is that the person your Module 2 persona describes?:** The pain is carried mostly by consumers with several open claims, and only partly by the shift-income consumer your persona describes. So the match is partial. A consumer with two or more open claims, often on variable income, using the portal on a phone.

- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** The behaviour change I expect is that consumers with several claims and variable income finish the journey with a plan they can afford, so fewer of them leave or call, and fewer plans break. The pack has no recovery amounts, fees or client contract data, so the mapping to retention, revenue and churn is a logic chain with an illustrative size, not a forecast.

Expected behaviour change
More of Mara’s segment complete the middle steps. Her segment leaves at 24.8% at affordability, 28.1% at documents and 30.1% at plan review. The target is that these leave rates move toward the one-claim, non-variable group (16.0%, 18.3%, 13.2%).
Fewer calls for answers the portal should give. The main reasons are “what if I miss a payment” (18.6%), “can claims be combined” (17.3%) and “why income is asked” (16.2%).
Plans chosen with real income in mind. Missed payments in her segment should fall from 24.9% toward the 16.5% of the reference group.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Primary signal: the share of Mara’s segment (2+ open claims, variable income) who reach the plan review screen. It is 44.5% today, against 63.0% for the one-claim, non-variable reference group.

It is the product of the three middle steps: 82.3% get past plan options, 75.2% past affordability and 71.9% past documents. The agreement rate follows from it (44.5% × 69.9% = 31.1%).
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Guardrail: the 60-day plan hold rate, meaning the share of new agreements with no missed payment in the first 60 days. It is 81.0% today (3,115 of 3,846), and it must not fall below that. It is the same measure as the 19.0% missed-payment rate, framed so that a drop is the warning sign.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** The decision window is 12 weeks of data on the primary signal, with a staged scale decision at week 12 and the full quality confirmation at about week 20. All thresholds below are my proposals, not numbers from the pack.

Assumptions
Test design: A control group is needed. A before-and-after comparison can’t separate your change from normal month-to-month noise. I assume a 50/50 split.
Volume: Mara’s segment gets about 1,007 starters a month, about 250 a week, so about 125 a week per arm.
Pilot size: The pack leaves the German pilot segment size open. If your pilot is smaller, every timeline below stretches.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on N-01’s account of leaving at the income question (“I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month”) and on 31.1% of consumers with two or more open claims and variable income reaching an agreement, against 54.7% for one claim and a non-variable income, I believe that clarifying what each ask means and letting these consumers handle their claims in one plan will result in more of them reaching plan review, as measured by a 9-point increase in that rate (44.5% to about 54%). I will protect the 60-day plan hold rate (81.0% overall, 83.5% for the one-claim, non-variable group) and will make a go/no-go decision after 12 weeks, with the hold rate confirmed at about week 20.
